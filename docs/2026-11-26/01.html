The Baker's Unexpected Inheritance面包师意外的遗产

A humble baker inherits a distant castle and must fight to keep its hidden treasure.

In the small town of Ashford, Tom Baker ran a modest bakery. His days were straightforward: wake before dawn, knead dough, and fill the oven with bread. He was a sensible man, not prone to grand dreams. His sole companion was a jolly old dog named Biscuit. Tom was fond of his simple life, but a nagging worry about money often intruded on his peace. The town's economy was in a recession, and every penny counted.

One crisp morning, a letter arrived with a strange seal. It was from a lawyer in the distant Isle of Skye. The letter stated that Tom's late uncle, a missionary who had worked abroad for years, had left him a property. Tom had to produce a certificate of identity to claim it. He was utterly surprised, as he barely remembered his uncle.

Tom decided to relocate to the isle to see his inheritance. He packed a crate with his few belongings and entrusted his bakery to his nephew, Ben. The journey was long, and the terrain grew rough and wild. He had to crawl over rocky paths at times. A sudden drought had made the land dry, and the air was thick with dust. Tom felt a lump in his throat as he finally saw the ancient castle.

The castle was a medieval gaol turned into a grand home. Its throne room was vast, with a high ceiling that seemed to echo with a chorus of ghosts. Tom felt a shadow of doubt. The place was huge and cold. He noticed a strange pyramid of old books in the corner. A brass pan hung over the fireplace, and the air smelled of herbs and timber.

Tom explored the castle's many rooms. In the kitchen, he found an old oven and a closet full of spices. The pantry had a crate of apples and a jar of herbs. He decided to fry some eggs for dinner. As he cooked, he felt a sharp pain in his side, an acute ache that made him wince. He sat down, feeling a bit ill.

The next day, Tom met the castle's caretaker, a lass named Elsie. She was intelligent and had a quick wit. She told him about the castle's history and the local scandal. A wealthy developer wanted to buy the land and build a franchise hotel. Elsie was accountable for the castle's upkeep, and she feared the developer's plans would degrade the area's beauty.

Tom was intrigued by a locked room in the tower. Elsie said the key was lost, but Tom was determined. He used a heavy iron bar to poke the door open. Inside, he found a strange capsule, like a time capsule, filled with old letters and a small dye-stained cloth. The letters revealed a hidden treasure, namely a set of medieval coins.

Tom felt a thrill of excitement. The coins could revive his bakery and save the castle. But he needed to be careful. He had to deduct the cost of any repairs from the treasure's value. The lawyer explained that the coins belonged to a treasury that had been lost for centuries. Tom had to allot a portion to the local council.

A sudden twist came when the developer, a man named Mr. Crale, arrived. He claimed the treasure was his, based on a trivial old document. Tom felt a bout of anger. He scolded Crale for his greed. Crale threatened to inflict legal trouble. Tom knew he had to be straightforward and honest, and he refused to be bullied.

Tom sought help from the local historical society. They were fascinated by the find and offered to help. A kind woman named Mrs. Pye, who was in charge of Medicare for the elderly, also offered support. She had a deep compassion for Tom's situation. She helped him weave through the complex laws. Tom felt he could entrust his case to these new friends.

The legal battle was a torture for Tom. He had to flee from reporters who intruded on his privacy. He felt like a knight fighting a dragon. But he found sanctuary in his love for baking. He used the castle's oven to bake biscuits for the villagers, who loved his crisp and sweet treats. The smell of fresh bread brought the community together.

Tom discovered a hidden garden with rare herbs and a small sanctuary for beetles. He decided to dye some cloth with natural colors and sell it to raise funds. He had to plough through paperwork, but he was accountable for every penny. The intrinsic value of the castle was not just in its treasure, but in its history and beauty.

The judge ruled in Tom's favor. Mr. Crale's claim was superficial and full of lies. Tom was hereby declared the rightful owner of the castle and its treasure. He felt a great weight lift from his shoulders. He could now revive his bakery and turn the castle into a museum. He would dwell in the castle and share its story with the world.

Tom returned to Ashford a happy man. He paid off his debts and even helped his nephew Ben. He had a new appreciation for life's simple joys. He warned others to beware of greed and to cherish what truly matters. His story was a reminder that even the most trivial beginning can lead to a great adventure.

Back at the bakery, Tom tried to reproduce his uncle's old recipes, but the results were a disaster. The department of health sent a notice about a strange ail that affected his customers. To keep a brisk tempo, he added a sprinkle of clay powder to the dough, but it was a slap in the face to his pride. He hired an ethnic baker who spoke a different pronoun, and together they achieved a feat of logistics: a quart of perfect dough. The aesthetic connotation of the bakery improved, and Tom felt inclined to smile again.

Tom also had to drain the old castle's flooded cellar, a task that took days. He realized the treasure was not an illusion but a real chance to rebuild his life. With the cellar dry, he stored his baking supplies there and felt ready for anything.
